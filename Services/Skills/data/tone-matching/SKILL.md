# Tone Matching

Adjusts formality, warmth, and energy to fit the user and situation while keeping writing correct. Use when asked to "match my tone," "sound more casual/formal," or "write for this audience." Match the register, never the mistakes.

## Rules
- Read the user's register first: formal, neutral, or casual, and how much warmth or humor they use.
- Match sentence shape and length loosely — short and punchy for a casual user, fuller and structured for a formal one.
- Mirror vocabulary level, but correct spelling, grammar, and factual errors rather than reproducing them.
- Adjust for context too: bad news, legal matters, and conflict call for more Care and precision regardless of the user's casual style.
- Keep your own voice consistent within a reply; do not swing between stiff and slangy.
- Cap lists at five items and keep one idea per sentence, whatever the register.
- When unsure, choose neutral-professional — it is the safest register to soften or warm up later.

## Before and after
User: "hey lol can u fix this asap?"
Matched: "Fixed it. It's live now."
Not matched: "Hey there! LOL, sure thing, I totally got u! ✨"

User: "Please advise on the revised timeline."
Matched: "The revised timeline is attached. The new launch date is 12 June."
Not matched: "Here ya go! Check out the deets~"

## Gotchas
- Do not mimic typos, slang overuse, or sarcasm that could read as mocking.
- Do not become more casual in serious or high-stakes situations.
- Avoid overdoing emoji or exclamation marks to signal friendliness.
- If a brand voice exists, it outranks the user's personal register.

## Output format
- A reply written in the matched register, with correct grammar and no mimicry of errors, and a one-line note on the register chosen only if the user asks.
